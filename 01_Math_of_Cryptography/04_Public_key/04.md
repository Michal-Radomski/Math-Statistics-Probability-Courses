**Fermat’s Little Theorem** is a fundamental building block of modern number theory. Discovered by the French mathematician
Pierre de Fermat in 1640 (and distinguished from his famous "Last Theorem"), it describes a predictable, repeating pattern
that prime numbers create when used with exponents and modular arithmetic.

_(Note: If you were just looking at RSA encryption, this theorem is actually the mathematical secret sauce that makes RSA
work!)_

---

## The Theorem Explained

There are two common ways to write Fermat's Little Theorem:

### Form 1 (The Remainder Form)

If $p$ is a prime number, and $a$ is any integer that is not divisible by $p$, then raising $a$ to the power of $p-1$ will
always leave a remainder of **1** when divided by $p$:

$$a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod p$$

### Form 2 (The General Form)

For _any_ integer $a$ (even if it is divisible by $p$) and any prime number $p$, raising $a$ to the power of $p$ leaves the
exact same remainder as $a$ itself when divided by $p$:

$$a^p \equiv a \pmod p$$

---

## A Quick Example

Let's test Form 1 using small numbers:

- Let our prime number be **$p = 5$**.
- Let our integer be **$a = 3$** (3 is not divisible by 5).

According to the theorem, $3^{5-1}$ divided by 5 should leave a remainder of 1. Let's check the math:

1. $p - 1 = 4$
2. $3^4 = 81$
3. $81 \div 5 = 16$ with a remainder of **1** ($16 \times 5 + 1 = 81$).

It works! No matter how large the prime number is, this neat cyclical property holds true.

---

## Why Is It So Important?

1. **Primality Testing:** Before computers can use huge prime numbers for encryption (like in RSA), they need a fast way to
   check if a massive random number is actually prime. Fermat's Little Theorem provides a quick test: if a number fails this
   formula, it is definitely _not_ prime.
2. **The Backbone of RSA:** When you encrypt and decrypt a message in RSA using the formula $M^{ed} \equiv M \pmod n$,
   Fermat's Little Theorem (and its broader cousin, Euler's Totient Theorem) is the exact mathematical proof that guarantees
   your encrypted message will successfully turn back into your original text when the private key is applied.
