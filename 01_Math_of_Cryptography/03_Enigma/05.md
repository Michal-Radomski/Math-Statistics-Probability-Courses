The **Bomba** (developed by Polish cryptanalysts) and the **Bombe** (developed later by British cryptanalysts at Bletchley
Park) were groundbreaking electromechanical devices. Their sole purpose was to automate the grueling process of finding the
daily secret settings—such as the order of the rotors and the plugboard connections—used by the German Enigma machine.

---

## 1. The Polish _Bomba_ (1938)

Before World War II, Marian Rejewski and his team faced an Enigma machine with rapidly changing rotor configurations. To
avoid testing every possibility by hand, they built the _Bomba_.

- **The Setup:** The Polish Bomba consisted of six Enigma-like units chained together.
- **How it Worked:** It exploited a specific German procedure where operators typed the message key twice at the start of a
  transmission (e.g., `ABC ABC`). Rejewski used permutation group theory to track these repeating patterns.
- **The Logic:** The machine cycled through possible rotor orders. When the electrical circuits in the Bomba found a match
  that aligned with the repeating double-letters, the drums stopped spinning, alerting the cryptanalysts to a potential rotor
  setting.

---

## 2. The British Turing-Welchman _Bombe_ (1939–1940)

When the Germans made Enigma much harder by introducing more rotors and daily plugboard settings, the Polish Bomba was no
longer enough. Alan Turing, joined later by Gordon Welchman, completely redesigned the concept to handle these complex new
variables.

Instead of trying to brute-force every single possibility from scratch, the British Bombe used **"cribs"**—educated guesses
about what a specific intercepted message might contain (such as "WETTER" for weather, or standard naval reports).

- **Simulating Multiple Enigmas:** A single Bombe contained dozens of rotating drums that mimicked the behavior of multiple
  Enigma machines wired together in a massive electrical circuit.
- **The Power of Contradiction:** Turing's machine didn't look for the right answer directly; instead, it searched for
  **logical contradictions**.
- **Elimination at Speed:** If a guessed crib word conflicted with the electrical logic of the machine (for example, if a
  letter tried to connect to itself on the plugboard, which was impossible on a real Enigma), the Bombe instantly detected a
  short-circuit, rejected that configuration, and moved on.
- **The Result:** When the drums stopped spinning, it meant the machine had found a rare configuration that _did not_ result
  in a logical contradiction. Cryptanalysts could then take those settings, plug them into a replica Enigma, and read the
  decrypted German message in minutes.
