Breaking the Enigma cipher was one of the most remarkable intelligence and scientific achievements of World War II. It
combined advanced mathematics, pioneering early computing, espionage, and the exploitation of human error.

The effort was a collaborative triumph, beginning with Polish mathematicians and dramatically expanded by British
cryptanalysts at Bletchley Park.

---

## 1. The Polish Foundation

In the 1930s, Polish military intelligence realized that Germany's commercial Enigma machine posed a major security threat. A
team of brilliant young mathematicians—led by **Marian Rejewski**, **Jerzy Różycki**, and **Henryk Zygalski**—managed to
mathematically reconstruct the inner wiring of the military Enigma without ever seeing one.

- **Exploiting Flawed Procedures:** Rejewski realized that German operators transmitted the message key (a 3-letter setting
  unique to each message) twice at the very beginning of every transmission. He used advanced permutation group theory to
  track these repeating patterns.
- **The Polish Bomba:** The Poles built an electromechanical device called the _Bomba_ (a precursor to Turing's machine) to
  automate the testing of rotor settings.
- **Sharing Secrets:** Just weeks before the German invasion of Poland in 1939, the Polish team handed over their research,
  reconstructed Enigma replicas, and blueprints to British and French intelligence.

---

## 2. Alan Turing and the British Bombe

When war broke out, the German military continuously increased the complexity of Enigma by adding more rotors and daily
plugboard settings. British mathematician **Alan Turing** and his colleagues at Bletchley Park took the Polish foundation and
scaled it up exponentially.

- **The Turing-Welchman Bombe:** Turing designed a massive electromechanical machine called the Bombe. Enhanced by Gordon
  Welchman’s "diagonal board," the Bombe didn't try every possible combination brute-force style. Instead, it searched for
  logical contradictions based on **"cribs"**—known or guessed phrases likely to appear in a message, such as weather
  reports, administrative updates, or standard sign-offs.
- **Automated Deduction:** When a drum rotated into a configuration that produced a logical impossibility, the Bombe
  eliminated thousands of bad settings instantly, drastically cutting down the time needed to find the correct day's key.

---

## 3. Human Error and Captured Hardware

Even with the Bombes running day and night, cryptanalysis relied heavily on operational weaknesses and physical captures:

- **Operator Laziness:** German operators frequently reused code keys, picked predictable sequences (like "ABC" or
  consecutive keyboard rows), or sent messages containing personal details or predictable weather forecasts at the exact same
  time every day.
- **Capturing Codebooks:** Allied naval forces bravely boarded captured German weather ships, surface vessels, and U-boats
  (most famously U-110 and U-559) to seize actual Enigma machines, codebooks, and short-signal tables. These physical
  captures provided the vital daily settings needed to keep the Bombes effective.
