In combinatorics (the branch of mathematics focused on counting and arranging), **permutations**, **variations**, and
**combinations** are the three core ways to group and order items. The main differences come down to two simple questions:

1. **Does the order of the items matter?**
2. **Are we using all the items or just a subset of them?**

---

### 1. Permutations (Order Matters, All Items)

A **permutation** is an arrangement of _all_ the items in a set where **order matters**.

- **The Concept:** If you have 3 books ($A, B, C$) and you want to arrange all of them on a shelf, the arrangement $ABC$ is
  completely different from $BCA$.
- **The Formula:** The number of ways to arrange $n$ items is $n$ factorial ($n!$).

$$P = n!$$

- **Example:** If you have 4 different books, how many different ways can you line them up on a shelf?

$$4! = 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1 = 24 \text{ ways}$$

---

### 2. Variations (Order Matters, Subset of Items)

A **variation** (often called a $k$-permutation or an arrangement) is a selection of a _subset_ ($r$) of items from a larger
set ($n$) where **order matters**.

- **The Concept:** This is like picking a 1st, 2nd, and 3rd place winner out of 10 runners. Finishing 1st is very different
  from finishing 3rd.
- **The Formula:**

$$V(n, r) = \frac{n!}{(n - r)!}$$

- **Example:** Out of a club of 10 people, how many ways can you choose a **President, Vice President, and Treasurer**?
  (Order matters because the roles are distinct).

$$V(10, 3) = \frac{10!}{(10 - 3)!} = \frac{10!}{7!} = 10 \times 9 \times 8 = 720 \text{ ways}$$

---

### 3. Combinations (Order Doesn't Matter, Subset of Items)

A **combination** is a selection of a subset ($r$) of items from a larger set ($n$) where **order does not matter**.

- **The Concept:** This is like drawing cards from a deck or picking lottery numbers. If your lottery numbers are 3, 12, 45,
  it doesn't matter which order they are drawn in; you still win.
- **The Formula:**

$$\binom{n}{r} = \frac{n!}{r!(n - r)!}$$

- **Example:** You have 5 different pizza toppings and want to pick **2 toppings** for your slice. Does the order you pick
  them matter? No, pepperoni and mushroom is the same as mushroom and pepperoni.

$$\binom{5}{2} = \frac{5!}{2!(5 - 2)!} = 10 \text{ unique combinations}$$

---

### Quick Comparison Table

| Concept          | Order Matters? | Items Used   | Formula               | Typical Example                         |
| ---------------- | -------------- | ------------ | --------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Permutations** | Yes            | All ($n$)    | $n!$                  | Arranging books on a shelf              |
| **Variations**   | Yes            | Subset ($r$) | $\frac{n!}{(n-r)!}$   | Awarding 1st, 2nd, and 3rd place medals |
| **Combinations** | No             | Subset ($r$) | $\frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}$ | Choosing a poker hand from a deck       |
