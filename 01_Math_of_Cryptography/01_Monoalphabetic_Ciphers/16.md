A **commuting cipher** (more commonly referred to in cryptography as a **commutative cipher** or **commutative encryption
scheme**) is a special type of encryption algorithm where the order in which multiple keys are applied does not affect the
final ciphertext or the ability to decrypt it.

In standard encryption, if you encrypt a message ($m$) with Key 1 ($E_{k_1}$) and then encrypt it again with Key 2
($E_{k_2}$), you must usually decrypt them in the exact reverse order. In a commutative cipher, the operations can commute.

---

### How It Works Mathematically

For two different encryption keys, $k_1$ and $k_2$, a cipher is commutative if applying them in either sequence yields the
exact same ciphertext:

$$E_{k_1}(E_{k_2}(m)) = E_{k_2}(E_{k_1}(m))$$

Similarly, when it comes to decryption, the keys can be applied in any order to recover the original plaintext message ($m$):

$$D_{k_1}(D_{k_2}(E_{k_1}(E_{k_2}(m)))) = m$$

---

### Key Examples

1. **The SRA Cipher (Shamir-Rivest-Adleman):** Developed by the same minds behind RSA, the SRA cipher is a classic example of
   a commutative encryption scheme. It relies on modular arithmetic (similar to RSA, but where both parties use private keys
   to encrypt and decrypt data).
2. **Pohlig-Hellman Exponential Cipher:** Another classic scheme based on the difficulty of solving discrete logarithms over
   a prime modulus. Like SRA, it allows multiple secret keys to be layered and removed in any order.
3. **Additive Stream Ciphers:** Certain stream ciphers (such as those using simple XOR operations with independent
   keystreams) can naturally commute because combining independent keystreams operates independently of sequence.

---

### Practical Use Cases

Commutative ciphers are rarely used for standard data encryption (like securing web traffic) because their mathematical
structures can introduce vulnerabilities if not implemented carefully. However, they are vital for specialized cryptographic
protocols:

- **Mental Poker:** This is a cryptographic concept that allows two or more players to play a fair game of cards over a
  network (without a trusted third party). Players can shuffle and encrypt a deck of cards sequentially. Because the ciphers
  commute, a player can remove their own encryption key from a card without needing to know or remove the other player's key
  first.
- **Anonymous Communication and Chaumian Mix Networks:** Commutative properties have historically inspired routing and
  multi-layer encryption designs where nodes can process or peel back layers of encryption independently.
- **Multi-party Authentication Protocols:** Scenarios where multiple authorities need to sign or encrypt data fields in a
  decentralized sequence without breaking downstream verification.
