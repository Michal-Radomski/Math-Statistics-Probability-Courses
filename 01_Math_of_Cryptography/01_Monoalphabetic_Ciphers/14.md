# Frequency Analysis in Ciphers

**Frequency Analysis** is a cryptanalysis technique used to break encrypted messages by studying how frequently letters,
symbols, or groups of letters appear in a ciphertext.

It is mainly used to break **substitution ciphers**, especially the Caesar cipher and monoalphabetic substitution cipher.

## 1\. What is Frequency Analysis?

In any language, certain letters appear more frequently than others.

For example, in English:

- E is the most frequently used letter.
- T is the second most frequent.
- A, O, I, N, S, H, and R are also common.
- Z, Q, X, and J are among the least frequent.

Frequency analysis uses these patterns to guess which ciphertext letters represent the original plaintext letters.

## 3\. How Frequency Analysis Works

The process involves the following steps:

1. **Collect ciphertext:** Obtain the encrypted message.
2. **Count letters:** Calculate how many times each letter appears.
3. **Calculate frequency:** Determine the percentage of each letter.
4. **Compare frequencies:** Compare ciphertext frequencies with normal English letter frequencies.
5. **Identify patterns:** Look for common letters, repeated pairs, and common words.
6. **Replace letters:** Make possible substitutions to recover the original message.

## 4\. Example of Frequency Analysis

Consider the ciphertext:

**KHOOR**

Using a Caesar cipher, each letter has been shifted three positions forward.

| Ciphertext | K   | H   | O   | O   | R   |
| ---------- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plaintext  | H   | E   | L   | L   | O   |

The decrypted message is:

**HELLO**

Frequency analysis can help identify that O appears twice and may represent a frequently used plaintext letter.

## 5\. Applications of Frequency Analysis

Frequency analysis is used in:

- Breaking substitution ciphers.
- Cryptanalysis and codebreaking.
- Studying classical encryption techniques.
- Analysing encrypted historical documents.
- Understanding weaknesses in traditional encryption systems.

## 6\. Advantages

- Simple to understand and implement.
- Does not require knowledge of the encryption key.
- Useful for breaking classical substitution ciphers.
- Helps identify patterns in encrypted messages.

## 7\. Disadvantages

- Less effective for short ciphertexts.
- Language frequency varies depending on the text.
- Does not work effectively against modern encryption algorithms.
- Advanced encryption techniques hide simple statistical patterns.

## 8\. Conclusion

Frequency analysis is an important technique in cryptography used to analyse and break classical substitution ciphers. It
works by comparing the frequency of letters in encrypted text with the frequency of letters in the original language.

**Key point:** Frequency analysis exploits the fact that some letters occur more frequently than others in a language.
