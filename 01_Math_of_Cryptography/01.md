While both **codes** and **ciphers** are methods used in cryptography to keep messages secret, they operate at fundamentally
different levels of language and structure.

Here is a breakdown of the differences between the two:

---

### 1. Code

A **code** operates at the level of **meaning (words, phrases, or ideas)**.

- **How it works:** A code replaces entire words, phrases, or sometimes full sentences with a completely different word,
  number, or symbol. Think of it like a translation dictionary between a secret language and a public one.
- **Example:** In a codebook, the word _"Apple"_ might mean _"Attack at dawn"_, and the number _"45"_ might mean _"Send
  reinforcements"_.
- **Key characteristic:** To understand or crack a code, you generally need a **codebook** that lists every assigned meaning.
  Without the codebook, the message is often entirely untranslatable because there is no logical mathematical pattern
  connecting the code to the original words.
- **Historical usage:** Military operations and diplomats historically used heavy codebooks to substitute common phrases or
  names of people and places.

---

### 2. Cipher

A **cipher** operates at the level of **individual letters or characters** (sometimes bits in modern computing).

- **How it works:** A cipher takes a message letter by letter and scrambles or replaces them according to a specific
  algorithm or mathematical rule (a key). The letters themselves are altered, but the underlying language remains the same.
- **Example (Substitution Cipher):** Shifting every letter of the alphabet forward by 1 (so $A$ becomes $B$, $B$ becomes $C$,
  etc.). The word _"CAT"_ becomes _"DBU"_.
- **Example (Transposition Cipher):** Rearranging the order of the letters without changing the letters themselves (e.g.,
  spelling a message backwards).
- **Key characteristic:** Ciphers rely on an **algorithm and a key**. Even if someone knows the method (cipher), they cannot
  read the message without knowing the specific key used to scramble it.
- **Modern usage:** Modern digital security (like HTTPS, encryption on your phone, and messaging apps) relies on advanced
  mathematical ciphers rather than codes.

---

### Summary Comparison Table

| Feature                  | Code                                                 | Cipher                                              |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Unit of Substitution** | Words, phrases, or whole ideas                       | Individual letters, characters, or bits             |
| **Tool Required**        | Codebook                                             | Algorithm and Key                                   |
| **Underlying Structure** | Arbitrary substitution (no direct mathematical link) | Systematic transformation (mathematical rules)      |
| **Example**              | "Bluebird" = "Send supplies"                         | $A \rightarrow D$, $B \rightarrow E$ (Caesar shift) |
