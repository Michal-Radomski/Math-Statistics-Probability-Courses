**Euler's notation** (also known as the **exponential form**) is one of the most powerful and elegant ways to represent
complex numbers. Instead of using rectangular coordinates ($a + bi$), it uses polar coordinates—describing a complex number
by its distance from the origin (magnitude) and its angle (argument).

---

### Euler's Formula

At the heart of this notation is **Euler's Formula**, which connects complex numbers, trigonometry, and exponential
functions:

$$e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$$

where:

- $e$ is the base of the natural logarithm ($e \approx 2.71828$),
- $\theta$ is the angle (in radians) measured counterclockwise from the positive real axis,
- $i$ is the imaginary unit ($i^2 = -1$).

---

### The Exponential Form of a Complex Number

Any complex number $z = a + bi$ can be written in polar form as $z = r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$, where $r$ is the magnitude
(or modulus) and $\theta$ is the argument. By applying Euler's formula, this simplifies directly into **Euler's/exponential
notation**:

$$z = r e^{i\theta}$$

- **Magnitude ($r$):** $r = \vert{}z\vert{} = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$
- **Angle ($\theta$):** $\theta = \text{arctan}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)$ (adjusted for the correct quadrant)

---

### Why Euler's Notation is Powerful

Multiplying and dividing complex numbers in rectangular form can be tedious. In exponential form, **multiplying and dividing
becomes effortless** using standard exponent rules ($e^x \cdot e^y = e^{x+y}$):

1. **Multiplication:**

$$(r_1 e^{i\theta_1}) \cdot (r_2 e^{i\theta_2}) = r_1 r_2 e^{i(\theta_1 + \theta_2)}$$

_(You multiply the magnitudes and add the angles!)_ 2. **Powers (De Moivre's Theorem):**

$$(r e^{i\theta})^n = r^n e^{in\theta}$$

3. **Euler's Identity:** If you set $\theta = \pi$ in Euler's formula, you get one of the most famous equations in all of
   mathematics, linking five fundamental mathematical constants:

$$e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$$
