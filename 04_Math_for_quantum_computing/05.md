**Bra-ket notation** (also known as **Dirac notation**) is a standard system for writing down vectors and linear algebra
operations, especially famous for its use in **quantum mechanics**.

Because quantum mechanics deals heavily with complex numbers, matrices, and conjugate transposes, bra-ket notation makes
complex math look much cleaner and easier to read!

---

### Where the Name Comes From

The name is a mathematical pun coined by physicist Paul Dirac. He took the English word **"bracket"** and split it into two
halves:

- **$\langle\text{bra}\vert{}$** (the left half)
- **$\vert{}\text{ket}\rangle$** (the right half)
- Put them together: **$\langle\text{bra} \mid \text{ket}\rangle$** = **bracket** (inner product)!

---

### 1. The Ket ($\vert{}\psi\rangle$) — Column Vectors

A **ket** is written as $\vert{}\psi\rangle$ (where $\psi$ is just a label, like a name). In linear algebra, a ket represents
a **column vector**.

If you have a 2D vector with complex number components, a ket looks like this:

$$\vert{}\psi\rangle = \begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \end{bmatrix}$$

---

### 2. The Bra ($\langle\psi\vert{}$) — Row Vectors & Conjugates

A **bra** is written as $\langle\psi\vert{}$. It represents a **row vector**, but with a crucial twist: it is the **conjugate
transpose** (Hermitian transpose) of its corresponding ket.

Remember our discussion on complex conjugates? To turn a ket into a bra, you:

1. Turn the column vector into a row vector.
2. Take the **complex conjugate** of every entry inside it.

If $\vert{}\psi\rangle = \begin{bmatrix} 1 + i \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$, then its bra is:

$$\langle\psi\vert{} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - i & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$

_(Notice how the $+i$ flipped to $-i$!)_

---

### 3. The Bracket ($\langle\phi\vert{}\psi\rangle$) — The Inner Product

When you combine a bra and a ket, you get a **bracket**, which represents the inner product (or dot product) of two vectors:

$$\langle\phi\vert{}\psi\rangle$$

If $\langle\phi\vert{}$ is a row vector and $\vert{}\psi\rangle$ is a column vector, multiplying them together results in a
**single scalar number** (which is often a complex number representing a probability amplitude in quantum physics).

---

### Why Do Physicists Love It?

In standard linear algebra, writing out matrix multiplications for high-dimensional complex vector spaces can get extremely
messy. Bra-ket notation hides the clumsy matrix indexing and lets you focus on the underlying physical states and operations.
