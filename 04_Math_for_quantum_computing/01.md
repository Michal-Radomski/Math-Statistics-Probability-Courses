A **complex number** is a number that can be expressed in the form:

$$z = a + bi$$

where:

- $a$ is the **real part** ($\text{Re}(z) = a$),
- $b$ is the **imaginary part** ($\text{Im}(z) = b$),
- $i$ is the imaginary unit, defined as $i^2 = -1$.

---

### What is the Complex Conjugate?

The **complex conjugate** of a complex number $z = a + bi$ is denoted as $\bar{z}$ (or sometimes $z^*$). To find the complex
conjugate, you simply **flip the sign of the imaginary part**:

$$\bar{z} = a - bi$$

#### Example:

- If $z = 3 + 4i$, then its complex conjugate is $\bar{z} = 3 - 4i$.
- If $z = 5 - 2i$, then its complex conjugate is $\bar{z} = 5 + 2i$.

---

### Geometric Interpretation

On the complex plane (Argand diagram), the complex conjugate $\bar{z}$ is the **reflection** of $z$ across the **real axis**
(the horizontal axis). If $z$ is located at coordinates $(a, b)$, its conjugate $\bar{z}$ is located at $(a, -b)$.

---

### Key Properties of the Complex Conjugate

1. **Sum with Conjugate:** Adding a number to its conjugate gives twice the real part.

$$z + \bar{z} = 2a$$

2. **Difference with Conjugate:** Subtracting the conjugate from the number gives twice the imaginary part multiplied by $i$.

$$z - \bar{z} = 2bi$$

3. **Product with Conjugate:** Multiplying a complex number by its conjugate always yields a non-negative real number equal
   to the square of its magnitude ($\vert{}z\vert{}^2$).

$$z \cdot \bar{z} = (a + bi)(a - bi) = a^2 - (bi)^2 = a^2 - b^2(-1) = a^2 + b^2 = \vert{}z\vert{}^2$$

_This property is extremely useful when dividing complex numbers, as it allows you to eliminate the imaginary unit from the
denominator._ 4. **Distributive Properties:**

$$\overline{z + w} = \bar{z} + \bar{w}$$

$$\overline{z \cdot w} = \bar{z} \cdot \bar{w}$$
