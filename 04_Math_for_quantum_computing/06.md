In quantum computing, **qubits** (quantum bits) are the basic units of information. Unlike classical bits, which must be
strictly $0$ or $1$, a qubit can exist in a **superposition** of both states at the same time.

Bra-ket notation makes writing down and manipulating these quantum states remarkably clean and intuitive.

---

### 1. The Computational Basis States ($\vert{}0\rangle$ and $\vert{}1\rangle$)

Just like classical bits have basic states of 0 and 1, qubits have two foundational basis states, represented as kets:

- **Ket Zero ($\vert{}0\rangle$):** Represents the quantum equivalent of classical `0`.

$$\vert{}0\rangle = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

- **Ket One ($\vert{}1\rangle$):** Represents the quantum equivalent of classical `1`.

$$\vert{}1\rangle = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

These two vectors form an **orthonormal basis**, meaning they are perpendicular to each other and each has a length of 1.

---

### 2. Representing Superposition

A qubit isn't just locked into $\vert{}0\rangle$ or $\vert{}1\rangle$. It can be in a combination of both—a
**superposition**.

We write a general qubit state $\vert{}\psi\rangle$ (psi) as a linear combination of $\vert{}0\rangle$ and $\vert{}1\rangle$:

$$\vert{}\psi\rangle = \alpha\vert{}0\rangle + \beta\vert{}1\rangle$$

Here, $\alpha$ (alpha) and $\beta$ (beta) are **complex numbers** called **probability amplitudes**.

If we translate this into column vector notation using our basis vectors:

$$\vert{}\psi\rangle = \alpha \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + \beta \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \alpha \\ \beta \end{bmatrix}$$

#### The Golden Rule of Probabilities:

When you measure a qubit, it collapses into either $0$ or $1$. The probabilities of getting each outcome are given by the
squared magnitudes of their amplitudes:

- Probability of measuring $0$ = $\vert{}\alpha\vert{}^2$
- Probability of measuring $1$ = $\vert{}\beta\vert{}^2$

Because the total probability of all possible outcomes must equal $1$ (100%), the amplitudes must satisfy:

$$\vert{}\alpha\vert{}^2 + \vert{}\beta\vert{}^2 = 1$$

---

### 3. The Bra Version ($\langle\psi\vert{}$)

If the ket represents our quantum state vector, what is its corresponding bra? By taking the **conjugate transpose**
(flipping it into a row vector and taking the complex conjugate of the amplitudes), we get:

$$\langle\psi\vert{} = \begin{bmatrix} \alpha^* & \beta^* \end{bmatrix}$$

---

### 4. Putting It Together: The Inner Product

If we want to find the inner product (overlap) between two quantum states $\langle\phi\vert{}$ and $\vert{}\psi\rangle$, we
multiply the row vector by the column vector:

$$\langle\phi\vert{}\psi\rangle = \begin{bmatrix} \phi_1^* & \phi_2^* \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \psi_1 \\ \psi_2 \end{bmatrix} = \phi_1^*\psi_1 + \phi_2^*\psi_2$$

If a state is normalized with itself, $\langle\psi\vert{}\psi\rangle = \vert{}\alpha\vert{}^2 + \vert{}\beta\vert{}^2 = 1$,
which proves the math behind total probability summing to 1!
