In probability and statistics, **Bayes' Theorem** is a powerful formula that allows us to update our beliefs or probabilities
when we get new evidence. It essentially lets you **reverse a conditional probability**: if you know the probability of $B$
given $A$, Bayes' Theorem helps you figure out the probability of $A$ given $B$.

---

## 1. The Formula

Mathematically, Bayes' Theorem is expressed as:

$$P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(B \mid A) \times P(A)}{P(B)}$$

To make sense of this, let's break down what each piece of the formula actually represents:

- **$P(A \mid B)$ (Posterior Probability):** What is the probability that event $A$ is true _given_ that we have observed
  event $B$? (This is what you are trying to calculate).
- **$P(B \mid A)$ (Likelihood):** If event $A$ is true, how likely is it that we would observe event $B$?
- **$P(A)$ (Prior Probability):** How likely was event $A$ _before_ we saw any new evidence (our baseline assumption)?
- **$P(B)$ (Marginal Probability / Evidence):** The total probability of observing event $B$ across all possible scenarios.

---

## 2. An Intuitive Example: Medical Testing

Imagine a rare disease that affects **1%** of the population ($P(A) = 0.01$).

There is a test for this disease with a **99% accuracy rate**:

- If you have the disease, the test correctly says positive 99% of the time ($P(B \mid A) = 0.99$).
- If you _don't_ have the disease, the test falsely says positive (a false positive) 2% of the time.

Suppose you take the test and **get a positive result**. Intuitively, you might think, _"The test is 99% accurate, so I
definitely have the disease!"_ But Bayes' Theorem reveals a surprising truth.

### Calculating with Bayes' Theorem:

1. **Prior $P(A)$:** Probability of having the disease = $0.01$ (1%).
2. **Likelihood $P(B \mid A)$:** Probability of a positive test if you have it = $0.99$.
3. **Total Evidence $P(B)$:** Total chance of testing positive (combining true positives and false positives)
   $\approx (0.01 \times 0.99) + (0.99 \times 0.02) = 0.0099 + 0.0198 = 0.0297$.

Plugging these into the formula:

$$P(\text{Disease} \mid \text{Positive}) = \frac{0.99 \times 0.01}{0.0297} \approx 0.33 \text{ (or } 33\% \text{)}$$

Even though the test is 99% accurate, because the disease is so rare, a positive result only means there is a **33% chance**
you actually have it. The prior probability (how rare the disease is) heavily influences the final outcome!

---

## 3. Why Bayes' Theorem Matters

Bayes' Theorem forms the backbone of **Bayesian statistics** and modern technology:

- **Spam Filters:** Email providers use Bayesian probability to calculate the chance that an incoming email is spam based on
  the words it contains.
- **Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning:** AI models constantly update their predictions and "learn" as they ingest
  new data.
- **Medical Diagnostics & Law:** Helping doctors and courts weigh evidence correctly without falling for common statistical
  traps like the "prosecutor's fallacy."
