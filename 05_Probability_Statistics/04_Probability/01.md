In probability, **independent events** are events where the outcome or occurrence of the first event has **no effect** on the
probability of the second event occurring.

When two things are independent, they have no memory of each other; the dice, coins, or numbers don't change their behavior
based on what just happened.

---

## Classic Examples of Independent Events

1. **Flipping a Coin and Rolling a Die**

- If you flip a coin and get heads, does that change the likelihood of rolling a 6 on a six-sided die? Not at all. The coin
  flip and the die roll are completely independent.

2. **Drawing Cards _With_ Replacement**

- If you draw a card from a standard 52-card deck, look at it, and then **put it back and shuffle**, your second draw is
  independent of your first draw. The odds reset to exactly 1 in 52.

---

## The Math: The Multiplication Rule

Because independent events don't influence each other, calculating the probability of both events happening together is
straightforward. You simply **multiply their individual probabilities**.

$$\text{Probability of both A and B} = P(A) \times P(B)$$

- **Example calculation:** What is the probability of flipping heads on a coin AND rolling a 6 on a die?
- Probability of heads ($P(A)$) = $\frac{1}{2}$
- Probability of rolling a 6 ($P(B)$) = $\frac{1}{6}$
- Combined probability = $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{12}$ (or about $8.3\%$)

---

## Independent vs. Dependent Events

To truly understand independence, it helps to look at its opposite—**dependent events**—where the outcome of the first event
_does_ change the odds of the second.

- **Independent (With Replacement):** Drawing a marble from a bag, looking at it, and putting it back. The totals stay the
  same.
- **Dependent (Without Replacement):** Drawing a marble from a bag and keeping it. Now, the total number of marbles changes,
  which alters the probability for the next draw.
