Email providers use a specialized application of Bayes' Theorem known as a **Naive Bayes Classifier** to catch junk emails.
Instead of looking at an email as a whole, the filter breaks it down word by word, calculating the mathematical probability
that an email is spam based on the vocabulary it contains.

---

## Step 1: Training the Filter (The Priors)

Before a filter can catch spam, it learns from history. It scans thousands of emails that users have previously marked as
**Spam** or **Ham** (legitimate mail).

By doing this, the system calculates two baseline prior probabilities:

1. **$P(\text{Spam})$:** What percentage of all incoming mail is typically spam? (e.g., 40%)
2. **$P(\text{Ham})$:** What percentage is legitimate mail? (e.g., 60%)

It also tracks how often specific words appear in spam versus ham. For example, how often does the word **"FREE"** show up in
junk mail compared to normal emails?

---

## Step 2: Applying Bayes' Theorem to a Single Word

Suppose an incoming email arrives containing the word **"FREE"**. You want to know: _Given that this email contains the word
"FREE", what is the probability that it is spam?_

Using Bayes' Theorem:

$$P(\text{Spam} \mid \text{"FREE"}) = \frac{P(\text{"FREE"} \mid \text{Spam}) \times P(\text{Spam})}{P(\text{"FREE"})}$$

Let's plug in some realistic numbers based on the filter's training data:

- **$P(\text{Spam})$:** 40% chance any random email is spam.
- **$P(\text{"FREE"} \mid \text{Spam})$:** 60% of all spam emails contain the word "FREE".
- **$P(\text{"FREE"})$:** 25% of _all_ emails (spam and ham combined) contain the word "FREE".

Plugging those into the formula:

$$P(\text{Spam} \mid \text{"FREE"}) = \frac{0.60 \times 0.40}{0.25} = \frac{0.24}{0.25} = 0.96 \text{ (or } 96\% \text{)}$$

Just by seeing the word "FREE", the filter calculates a **96% chance** that the email is junk!

---

## Step 3: Combining Multiple Words (The "Naive" Part)

Real emails contain sentences, not just single words. A spam filter evaluates multiple words (like "FREE", "WINNER",
"URGENT", and "INVOICE") all at once.

It is called **"Naive"** because it makes a simplifying assumption: it assumes that the presence of one word (like "FREE") is
completely independent of another word (like "WINNER"). Even though words in a sentence are actually dependent on each other,
this assumption makes the math fast enough to run instantly on billions of emails every day.

The filter multiplies the probabilities of all the individual words together to arrive at a final combined score. If the
final score crosses a certain threshold (say, 85% probability), the email gets automatically diverted straight to your spam
folder.
