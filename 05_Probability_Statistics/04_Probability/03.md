In probability and statistics, **tree diagrams** and **Venn diagrams** are two of the most powerful visual tools you can use.
While equations and numbers are great, diagrams let you actually _see_ how events unfold and how different groups relate to
one another.

---

## 1. What is a Tree Diagram?

A **tree diagram** is a branching visual tool used to map out all the possible outcomes of a **multi-step sequence of
events**. It gets its name because it starts from a single point (the trunk) and branches outward into multiple pathways,
looking like a tree on its side.

- **How it works:** Each branch represents a possible choice or outcome for a specific step. You write the probability of
  each outcome directly on its branch. To find the total probability of a specific path from start to finish, you
  **multiply** the probabilities along those branches.
- **When to use it:** Perfect for sequential events—like flipping a coin three times in a row, drawing two marbles from a bag
  without replacement, or tracking the outcomes of a tournament.

---

## 2. What is a Venn Diagram?

A **Venn diagram** uses overlapping circles (or other shapes) to show the logical relationships between different groups,
sets, or events.

- **How it works:**
- Each circle represents a specific set or category (e.g., people who play soccer vs. people who play basketball).
- The **overlapping region** (the intersection) represents items or outcomes that belong to _both_ groups at the same time.
- The space outside the circles represents items that belong to neither group.

- **When to use it:** Great for understanding set theory, finding the overlap between categories, and solving problems
  involving "AND" ($A \cap B$) versus "OR" ($A \cup B$) probabilities.

## Quick Comparison: Tree vs. Venn Diagrams

| Feature             | Tree Diagram                                     | Venn Diagram                                           |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Main Purpose**    | Tracking sequential, multi-step events over time | Showing relationships and overlaps between groups/sets |
| **Structure**       | Branching pathways growing from left to right    | Overlapping circles or closed shapes                   |
| **Key Math Action** | **Multiplication** along branches                | **Addition/Subtraction** of overlapping regions        |
| **Best Example**    | Flipping a coin, then rolling a die              | Students in a school who take art **and/or** music     |
