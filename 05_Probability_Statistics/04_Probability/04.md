In probability, **conditional probability** is the likelihood of an event occurring **given that another event has already
occurred**.

Instead of looking at the whole universe of possibilities, you restrict your focus to a specific subset (the condition) that
you now know is true. It is typically written as $P(A \mid B)$, which reads as _"the probability of event A given event B."_

---

## 1. How It Works (An Intuitive Example)

Imagine you are drawing a single card from a standard 52-card deck.

- **Unconditional Probability:** What is the probability that the card is a King?
- There are 4 Kings in a deck of 52, so $P(\text{King}) = \frac{4}{52} = \frac{1}{13}$.

- **Conditional Probability:** Suppose someone draws a card, looks at it, and tells you: _"It is a face card (Jack, Queen, or
  King)."_ Now, what is the probability that it is a **King** given that condition?
- Because you know it's a face card, your total possible universe shrinks from 52 down to **12 face cards**.
- Out of those 12 face cards, 4 are Kings.
- Therefore, the conditional probability is $P(\text{King} \mid \text{Face Card}) = \frac{4}{12} = \frac{1}{3}$.

---

## 2. The Formula

To calculate conditional probability mathematically, you take the probability of **both** events happening together and
divide it by the probability of the **given condition**:

$$P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \text{ and } B)}{P(B)}$$

_(Note: $P(B)$ cannot be zero, because the condition must actually be possible)._

- **Using our card example:**
- Probability of getting both a King and a Face Card ($P(A \text{ and } B)$) = $\frac{4}{52}$ (since all Kings are face
  cards).
- Probability of getting a Face Card ($P(B)$) = $\frac{12}{52}$.
- $P(\text{King} \mid \text{Face Card}) = \frac{4/52}{12/52} = \frac{4}{12} = \frac{1}{3}$.

---

## 3. Conditional Probability vs. Independent Events

Conditional probability is the exact reason why **dependent events** change their odds:

- **Independent Events:** Knowing event B happened tells you _nothing_ new about event A. (e.g., $P(A \mid B) = P(A)$).
- **Dependent Events:** Knowing event B happened _drastically changes_ the odds of event A. (e.g., drawing cards without
  replacement, medical testing, weather forecasting).
