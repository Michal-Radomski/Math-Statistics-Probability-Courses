In probability, **mutually exclusive events** are two or more events that **cannot happen at the same time**. If one event
occurs, the other is completely impossible for that trial or scenario.

On a Venn diagram, mutually exclusive events are represented as **two separate circles that do not overlap**, because they
share no common outcomes.

---

## Classic Examples of Mutually Exclusive Events

1. **Flipping a Coin**

- On a single coin flip, you can get heads or tails, but you can never get **both** at the same time.

2. **Rolling a Die**

- If you roll a standard six-sided die, you can roll a 2 or a 5, but you cannot roll a number that is both a 2 and a 5 on a
  single roll.

3. **Card Suits**

- Drawing a single card from a deck and having it be entirely a Heart or entirely a Spade. (A single card cannot belong to
  two different suits).

---

## The Math: The Addition Rule

Because mutually exclusive events cannot happen together, calculating the probability of **either** event happening is very
straightforward: you simply **add their individual probabilities together**.

$$\text{Probability of A or B} = P(A) + P(B)$$

- **Example calculation:** What is the probability of rolling either a 2 or a 5 on a single six-sided die?
- Probability of rolling a 2 ($P(A)$) = $\frac{1}{6}$
- Probability of rolling a 5 ($P(B)$) = $\frac{1}{6}$
- Combined probability = $\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$ (or about $33.3\%$)

---

## Mutually Exclusive vs. Non-Mutually Exclusive

To see the difference, look at what happens when events _can_ overlap:

- **Mutually Exclusive:** Rolling a 2 or rolling an even number? Wait—2 _is_ an even number! If you roll a 2, you satisfy
  both conditions. These are **not** mutually exclusive because they can happen together.
- **Non-Mutually Exclusive Formula:** If events can overlap, you have to subtract the probability of both happening at the
  same time: $P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \text{ and } B)$. For true mutually exclusive events,
  $P(A \text{ and } B)$ is simply zero.
