In statistics and probability, **populations** and **samples** are two fundamental concepts used to make sense of data.
Because it's usually impossible to measure every single thing in a massive group, statisticians rely on studying a smaller
piece to understand the whole.

---

## 1. What is a Population?

A **population** is the **entire group** of individuals, objects, or events that you want to study and draw conclusions
about.

- **Key trait:** It includes _every single member_ that fits your criteria.
- **Examples:**
- Every student currently enrolled in high school in the United States.
- All smartphones manufactured by a specific company in a given year.
- Every fish living in a particular lake.

When you calculate a measurement for an entire population (like the average height of every single high school student), it
is called a **parameter**.

---

## 2. What is a Sample?

A **sample** is a **smaller, manageable subset** of the population chosen to represent it. Instead of studying the whole
group, you collect data from this smaller group and use it to estimate what the whole population looks like.

- **Key trait:** It must be representative of the population (ideally chosen randomly) to avoid bias.
- **Examples:**
- Surveying 500 high school students across 10 random states instead of all millions of students.
- Testing 50 smartphones off the assembly line for battery life.
- Catching and measuring 30 fish from the lake.

When you calculate a measurement for a sample (like the average height of those 500 surveyed students), it is called a
**statistic**.

---

## Quick Comparison: Population vs. Sample

| Feature              | Population                                                               | Sample                                           |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Definition**       | The complete set of all items or people under study                      | A subset of the population chosen for the study  |
| **Feasibility**      | Often impossible, too expensive, or takes too much time to measure fully | Practical, cost-effective, and fast to collect   |
| **Measurement Name** | **Parameter** (e.g., population mean $\mu$)                              | **Statistic** (e.g., sample mean $\bar{x}$)      |
| **Goal**             | To know the exact truth about the entire group                           | To estimate or infer truths about the population |
