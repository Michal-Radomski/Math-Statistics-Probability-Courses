A **Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF)** is a fundamental concept in probability and statistics that describes the
probability that a random variable $X$ will take a value **less than or equal to** a specific number $x$.

While a Probability Density Function (PDF) tells you the _density_ or likelihood of a variable landing around a specific
point, a CDF accumulates all those probabilities from the very beginning up to that point.

---

### 1. The Core Definition

Mathematically, the CDF of any random variable $X$ (whether discrete or continuous) is denoted by $F(x)$ and defined as:

$$F(x) = P(X \le x)$$

- If you plug in a value $x$, the CDF gives you a cumulative percentage or probability (expressed as a number between $0$ and
  $1$).
- As you move from left to right along the number line, the CDF always builds up or stays flat, eventually reaching $1$ (or
  100%) as it accounts for all possible outcomes.

---

### 2. Key Properties of a CDF

No matter what kind of distribution you are working with, every valid CDF shares these four mathematical rules:

1. **Bounded Between 0 and 1:** The values of a CDF can never be less than $0$ (0%) or greater than $1$ (100%).

$$0 \le F(x) \le 1$$

2. **Non-Decreasing:** As $x$ increases, the CDF can go up or stay flat, but it can never go down. (Probabilities accumulate;
   they can't un-happen).
3. **Limits at Infinity:**

- As $x$ approaches negative infinity ($\lim_{x \to -\infty} F(x) = 0$), the probability of being less than an infinitely
  small number is $0$.
- As $x$ approaches positive infinity ($\lim_{x \to \infty} F(x) = 1$), the probability of being less than an infinitely
  large number is $1$.

4. **Right-Continuous:** CDFs are continuous from the right (especially relevant for discrete distributions, where the graph
   steps up like a staircase).

---

### 3. Connection to PDFs (For Continuous Variables)

If you have a Probability Density Function ($f(x)$), the CDF is simply the **integral** (the accumulated area under the
curve) of that PDF from negative infinity up to your value $x$:

$$F(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{x} f(t) \, dt$$

Conversely, if you take the derivative of a CDF, you get back the PDF.

---

### 4. Why Are CDFs Useful?

- **Finding Range Probabilities Easily:** Instead of integrating a PDF across a complex range every time, if you know the
  CDF, finding the probability that $X$ falls between two points $a$ and $b$ is a simple subtraction:

$$P(a < X \le b) = F(b) - F(a)$$

- **Calculating Percentiles:** CDFs make it easy to find statistical percentiles (like the median or the 95th percentile).
  For example, the 95th percentile is simply the value of $x$ where $F(x) = 0.95$.
