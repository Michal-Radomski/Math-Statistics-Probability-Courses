A **Poisson distribution** is a discrete probability distribution that models the **number of times an event occurs within a
specified interval** of time, space, volume, or distance.

Unlike the binomial distribution (which counts successes in a fixed number of trials), the Poisson distribution is used when
there is **no fixed number of trials**, but rather a known average rate at which events happen.

---

### Key Characteristics & Assumptions

For a situation to follow a Poisson distribution, it typically meets these conditions:

1. **Independence:** The occurrence of one event does not affect or change the probability that another event will occur.
2. **Constant Rate:** The average rate ($\lambda$) at which events occur is constant over the entire interval.
3. **Non-Simultaneity:** Two events cannot occur at the exact same infinitesimal instant; they happen one after another.

---

### The Poisson Formula

To find the exact probability of getting $k$ events in a given interval, we use the Poisson probability mass function:

$$P(X = k) = \frac{\lambda^k e^{-\lambda}}{k!}$$

Where:

- $X$ = the random variable representing the number of events
- $k$ = the exact number of occurrences you want to find the probability for ($0, 1, 2, 3, \dots$)
- $\lambda$ (lambda) = the **expected (average) number** of occurrences in the given interval
- $e$ = Euler's number, a mathematical constant approximately equal to $2.71828$
- $k!$ = factorial of $k$ (e.g., $3! = 3 \times 2 \times 1 = 6$)

---

### Unique Feature: Mean Equals Variance

A fascinating mathematical property of the Poisson distribution is that **its mean and variance are identical**:

- **Expected Value (Mean):** $\mu = \lambda$
- **Variance:** $\sigma^2 = \lambda$
- **Standard Deviation:** $\sigma = \sqrt{\lambda}$

This means if you know the average rate of an event, you automatically know its variance and standard deviation!

---

### Real-World Example: Customer Arrivals

Imagine you run a coffee shop, and on average, **3 customers arrive every 10 minutes** ($\lambda = 3$).

If you want to calculate the exact probability that **precisely 5 customers** walk in during the next 10-minute window
($k = 5$):

$$P(X = 5) = \frac{3^5 \cdot e^{-3}}{5!}$$

$$P(X = 5) = \frac{243 \cdot 0.0498}{120} \approx 0.1008 \text{ (or about 10.1\%)}$$
