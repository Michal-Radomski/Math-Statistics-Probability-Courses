For a **Normal Distribution**, there are two important mathematical functions:

1. **PDF (Probability Density Function)**
2. **CDF (Cumulative Distribution Function)**

Both describe the probability distribution, but they serve different purposes.

## 1\. PDF – Probability Density Function

The PDF describes the probability density at a particular value $x$.

The mathematical formula is:

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}}
}
$$

Where:

- $x$ = Value being evaluated.
- $\mu$ = Mean of the distribution.
- $\sigma$ = Standard deviation.
- $\sigma^2$ = Variance.
- $e$ = Euler's number (approximately 2.71828).
- $\pi$ = Pi (approximately 3.14159).

**Important:** PDF does not directly give the probability of getting exactly $x$. For a continuous distribution:

$$
P(X=x)=0
$$

To calculate the probability between two values, integrate the PDF:

$$
P(a<X<b)=\int_a^b f(x)\,dx
$$

## 2\. CDF – Cumulative Distribution Function

The CDF calculates the probability that a random variable $X$ is less than or equal to a particular value $x$.

Its mathematical formula is:

$$
\boxed{
F(x)=P(X\leq x)=
\int_{-\infty}^{x} f(t)\,dt
}
$$

For the normal distribution:

$$
\boxed{
F(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
\int_{-\infty}^{x}
e^{-\frac{(t-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}}\,dt
}
$$

Using the standard normal CDF:

$$
F(x)=\Phi\left(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\right)
$$

## 3\. Difference between PDF and CDF

| Feature                | PDF                              | CDF                              |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Full name              | Probability Density Function     | Cumulative Distribution Function |
| Purpose                | Measures probability density     | Measures cumulative probability  |
| Output                 | Density                          | Probability (0 to 1)             |
| Mathematical operation | Density function                 | Integral of PDF                  |
| Typical use            | Understanding distribution shape | Calculating probabilities        |

## 4\. Relationship between PDF and CDF

The CDF is the integral of the PDF:

$$
\boxed{F(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}f(t)\,dt}
$$

The PDF is the derivative of the CDF:

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\frac{d}{dx}F(x)}
$$

This means:

- Integrating PDF gives CDF.
- Differentiating CDF gives PDF.

## 5\. Practical example

Suppose we have:

$$
X\sim N(100,15^2)
$$

Where:

- Mean = 100
- Standard deviation = 15

We want to calculate the probability that $X\leq120$.

First, calculate the Z-score:

$$
z=\frac{120-100}{15}=1.3333
$$

Then use the CDF:

$$
P(X\leq120)=\Phi(1.3333)
$$

$$
\boxed{P(X\leq120)\approx0.9088=90.88\%}
$$

**In summary:**

- **PDF:** How dense is the probability around a particular value?
- **CDF:** What is the accumulated probability up to a particular value?
