While the **expected value** ($E(X)$ or $\mu$) tells you the long-term average outcome of a discrete random variable, the
**variance** measure how spread out those outcomes are around that mean.

A low variance means the outcomes tend to stay close to the expected value, while a high variance means the outcomes can
deviate significantly from the mean.

---

### Formulas for Variance

Variance of a random variable $X$ is written as $\text{Var}(X)$ or $\sigma^2$.

#### 1. Conceptual Definition

By definition, variance is the **expected value of the squared deviations from the mean**:

$$\text{Var}(X) = E\left[(X - \mu)^2\right] = \sum (x - \mu)^2 \cdot P(X = x)$$

#### 2. Computational Formula (Easier for Calculations)

In practice, expanding that formula gives a much simpler calculation step:

$$\text{Var}(X) = E(X^2) - [E(X)]^2$$

where:

- $E(X^2) = \sum x^2 \cdot P(X = x)$ (square each outcome first, then take the expectation)
- $[E(X)]^2$ is the square of the mean you already calculated.

---

### Step-by-Step Example

Let's use a simple discrete random variable $X$ (e.g., rolling a fair 4-sided die):

- Outcomes ($x$): $1, 2, 3, 4$
- Each has probability $P(X = x) = \frac{1}{4} = 0.25$

#### Step 1: Calculate the Mean $E(X)$

$$E(X) = (1 \times 0.25) + (2 \times 0.25) + (3 \times 0.25) + (4 \times 0.25)$$

$$E(X) = 0.25 + 0.50 + 0.75 + 1.00 = 2.5$$

#### Step 2: Calculate $E(X^2)$

Square each $x$ value before multiplying by its probability:

$$E(X^2) = (1^2 \times 0.25) + (2^2 \times 0.25) + (3^2 \times 0.25) + (4^2 \times 0.25)$$

$$E(X^2) = (1 \times 0.25) + (4 \times 0.25) + (9 \times 0.25) + (16 \times 0.25)$$

$$E(X^2) = 0.25 + 1.00 + 2.25 + 4.00 = 7.5$$

#### Step 3: Apply the Computational Formula

$$\text{Var}(X) = E(X^2) - [E(X)]^2$$

$$\text{Var}(X) = 7.5 - (2.5)^2 = 7.5 - 6.25 = 1.25$$

---

### Standard Deviation ($\sigma$)

Because variance squares the original units (e.g., "dollars squared" or "meters squared"), we take the positive square root
to get back to the original unit. This is the **standard deviation**:

$$\sigma = \sqrt{\text{Var}(X)} = \sqrt{1.25} \approx 1.118$$

---

### Key Properties of Variance

- **Non-Negativity:** $\text{Var}(X) \ge 0$ always, because squared deviations cannot be negative.
- **Adding a Constant:** $\text{Var}(X + c) = \text{Var}(X)$ — shifting all outcomes by a constant value changes the mean,
  but does not alter the spread.
- **Multiplying by a Constant:** $\text{Var}(aX) = a^2 \text{Var}(X)$ — scaling outcomes by $a$ scales the variance by $a^2$.
