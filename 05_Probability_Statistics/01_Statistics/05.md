The **Coefficient of Variation (CV)** is a standardized measure of how spread out data is relative to its mean. It is also
known as the **relative standard deviation (RSD)**.

While standard deviation tells you the absolute amount of spread in your data's original units, the coefficient of variation
expresses that spread as a **percentage** of the mean.

---

### Why Use the Coefficient of Variation?

The main benefit of the CV is that it allows you to **compare the variability of two or more datasets that have completely
different units or vastly different means**.

For example, comparing the variability of adult heights (measured in inches) versus adult weights (measured in pounds) using
standard deviation alone is misleading because the units and averages are on different scales. CV solves this by converting
everything into a dimensionless percentage.

---

### The Formula

The formula depends on whether you are looking at a population or a sample:

- **Population CV:**

$$CV = \left( \frac{\sigma}{\mu} \right) \times 100\%$$

_(Where $\sigma$ is the population standard deviation and $\mu$ is the population mean)_

- **Sample CV:**

$$CV = \left( \frac{s}{\bar{x}} \right) \times 100\%$$

_(Where $s$ is the sample standard deviation and $\bar{x}$ is the sample mean)_

---

### Example: Comparing Two Investments

Imagine you are looking at two different stocks:

- **Stock A:** Average price of $\$10$, with a standard deviation of $\$2$.
- **Stock B:** Average price of $\$1,000$, with a standard deviation of $\$50$.

If you only look at standard deviation, Stock B looks much more volatile ($\$50$ vs $\$2$). But let's calculate their
Coefficients of Variation:

- **CV of Stock A:**

$$\left(\frac{2}{10}\right) \times 100\% = 20\%$$

- **CV of Stock B:**

$$\left(\frac{50}{1000}\right) \times 100\% = 5\%$$

**Conclusion:** Relative to their average prices, Stock A actually has a much higher variability (20%) than Stock B (5%).
