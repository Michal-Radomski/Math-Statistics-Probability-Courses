**Skew** (or **skewness**) is a measure of the asymmetry of a probability distribution or dataset. While variance and
standard deviation tell you _how spread out_ your data is, skewness tells you _which direction_ the data leans.

In a perfectly symmetrical dataset (like a classic bell curve), the data mirrors itself evenly on both sides. When a dataset
is skewed, it is lopsided, with a longer "tail" stretching out in one direction.

---

### The Three Types of Skew

#### 1. Symmetrical (Zero Skew)

- **What it looks like:** The left and right sides are mirror images.
- **Key feature:** The **mean**, **median**, and **mode** are all right in the exact center.
- **Example:** Height or weight in a large, naturally distributed population.

#### 2. Right-Skewed (Positive Skew)

- **What it looks like:** The peak of the data is shifted to the left, and a long tail trails off to the **right**.
- **Key feature:** Because of the high-value outliers pulling the average up, the **mean is greater than the median**
  ($\text{Mean} > \text{Median}$).
- **Example:** Personal income. Most people earn within a certain range, but a small number of billionaires pull the average
  (mean) way up higher than the middle income (median).

#### 3. Left-Skewed (Negative Skew)

- **What it looks like:** The peak of the data is shifted to the right, and a long tail trails off to the **left**.
- **Key feature:** Because low-value outliers pull the average down, the **mean is less than the median**
  ($\text{Mean} < \text{Median}$).
- **Example:** Grades on an easy test. Most students score an A or B, but a few low scores pull the average down.

---

### Quick Comparison of Mean, Median, and Mode

| Skew Type                 | Relationship Between Averages               | Tail Direction                      |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Symmetrical**           | $\text{Mean} = \text{Median} = \text{Mode}$ | None (Balanced)                     |
| **Right (Positive) Skew** | $\text{Mode} < \text{Median} < \text{Mean}$ | Points to the right ($\rightarrow$) |
| **Left (Negative) Skew**  | $\text{Mean} < \text{Median} < \text{Mode}$ | Points to the left ($\leftarrow$)   |
