Here is a breakdown of **Range**, **Quantiles**, and the **Inter-Quartile Range (IQR)**, which are all measures used in
statistics to describe how spread out or dispersed a dataset is.

---

### 1. The Range

The range is the simplest measure of dispersion. It tells you the total span of your data from the lowest value to the
highest value.

- **Formula:**

$$\text{Range} = \text{Maximum Value} - \text{Minimum Value}$$

- **Example:** If the ages in a group range from $14$ to $18$, the range is:

$$\text{Range} = 18 - 14 = 4$$

- **Limitation:** Because it only looks at the two extreme values (the maximum and minimum), the range can be heavily skewed
  by a single outlier.

---

### 2. Quantiles

Quantiles are cut points that divide a dataset into equal-sized subgroups or intervals. Depending on how many sections you
divide the data into, quantiles have specific names:

- **Quartiles:** Divide data into **4** equal parts ($Q_1, Q_2, Q_3$).
- $Q_1$ (First Quartile): The 25th percentile (25% of data lies below this).
- $Q_2$ (Second Quartile): The 50th percentile, which is also the **median**.
- $Q_3$ (Third Quartile): The 75th percentile (75% of data lies below this).

- **Percentiles:** Divide data into **100** equal parts (e.g., scoring in the 90th percentile means you scored higher than
  90% of people).
- **Deciles:** Divide data into **10** equal parts.

---

### 3. Inter-Quartile Range (IQR)

The Inter-Quartile Range measures the spread of the **middle 50%** of your dataset. By focusing on the middle values, the IQR
completely ignores outliers and extreme high or low scores, making it a very reliable measure of spread.

- **Formula:**

$$\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$$

- **How to find it:**

1. Find the first quartile ($Q_1$), which marks the 25th percentile.
2. Find the third quartile ($Q_3$), which marks the 75th percentile.
3. Subtract $Q_1$ from $Q_3$.

- **Example:** If a dataset has a $Q_1$ of $15$ and a $Q_3$ of $17$, the IQR is:

$$\text{IQR} = 17 - 15 = 2$$
