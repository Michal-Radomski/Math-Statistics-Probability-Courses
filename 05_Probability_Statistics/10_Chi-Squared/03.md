A **Chi-Square Goodness of Fit Test** is a statistical hypothesis test used to determine whether a sample of categorical data
matches a theoretical or expected distribution. In short, it answers the question: _"How well does my observed data fit what
I expected to see?"_

---

### The Core Formula

The test relies on comparing your **Observed** frequencies ($O$) against your **Expected** frequencies ($E$) using this
formula:

$$\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E}$$

- **$O$ (Observed):** The actual counts you collected from your sample or experiment.
- **$E$ (Expected):** The counts you would expect to see if your null hypothesis were completely true.
- **$\sum$ (Sum):** You calculate this squared difference divided by the expected value for every category, then add them all
  together.

---

### Step-by-Step How It Works

1. **State the Hypotheses:**

- **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):** There is no significant difference between the observed and expected distributions (the data
  fits the model/theory).
- **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_a$):** There is a significant difference (the data does _not_ fit the model/theory).

2. **Calculate Expected Frequencies:** Based on your theory or probability model, determine what count belongs in each
   category.
3. **Compute the Test Statistic ($\chi^2$):** Apply the formula above for each category and sum the results. A larger
   $\chi^2$ value means a bigger discrepancy between what you saw and what you expected.
4. **Compare with Critical Value or Find the $p$-value:** Using the degrees of freedom ($df = k - 1$, where $k$ is the number
   of categories) and a chosen significance level (like $\alpha = 0.05$), you determine whether the result is extreme enough
   to reject the null hypothesis.

---

### A Quick Example: Testing a Die

Imagine you roll a 6-sided die **60 times** to check if it is fair. For a fair die, you expect each number (1 through 6) to
appear 10 times.

- **Expected ($E$) for each side:** $10$
- **Observed ($O$):** Let's say you roll:
- 1: 8 times
- 2: 12 times
- 3: 9 times
- 4: 11 times
- 5: 7 times
- 6: 13 times

You plug these into the $\chi^2$ formula to calculate the total discrepancy. If the sum is small and the $p$-value is high,
you conclude the die is likely fair. If the sum is very large, you have evidence the die might be weighted!
