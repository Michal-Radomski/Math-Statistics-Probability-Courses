While **regression** is the statistical tool used to find a relationship between variables, **interpolation** and
**extrapolation** are two different ways we use that relationship to make predictions.

To put it simply: regression builds the roadmap, and interpolation or extrapolation are how we use that map to find specific
locations.

---

### 1. Regression (The Model)

**Regression** is the mathematical process of analyzing data points to find a trend or equation (like a line of best fit). It
describes _how_ variables relate to each other so you can use them to make predictions.

- **Example:** Analyzing historical data of a city's temperature over the last 10 years to create an equation that links
  months of the year to average temperatures.

### 2. Interpolation (Filling in the Gaps)

**Interpolation** is the process of predicting a value **inside** the range of data points you already have. Because you have
data points on both sides of your estimate, interpolation is generally very reliable and accurate.

- **Example:** If you have temperature records for January ($40^\circ\text{F}$) and March ($50^\circ\text{F}$), using your
  regression model to estimate the temperature for February is **interpolation**.

### 3. Extrapolation (Venturing Into the Unknown)

**Extrapolation** is the process of predicting a value **outside** the range of your existing data points—extending the trend
line into the future or the past. Because you are guessing about what happens where you have no data, extrapolation can
become very risky and inaccurate very quickly.

- **Example:** Using your 10 years of temperature data to predict what the average temperature in that city will be in the
  year 2060 is **extrapolation**. (Unforeseen changes like climate shifts make this risky).

---

### Quick Comparison

| Feature                | Regression                                       | Interpolation                                                  | Extrapolation                                                     |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Primary Goal**       | Find the relationship/equation between variables | Estimate values **inside** the known data range                | Estimate values **outside** the known data range                  |
| **Risk / Uncertainty** | Low to moderate (depends on data fit)            | Very low (bounded by known data on both sides)                 | High (trends can change unexpectedly outside known bounds)        |
| **Analogy**            | Drawing the trend line on a map                  | Finding a town sitting right between two cities you've visited | Predicting what terrain looks like miles past the end of your map |
