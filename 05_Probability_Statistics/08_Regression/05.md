Not all relationships in the real world form a straight line. When data points curve upward or downward at an accelerating
rate—rather than increasing or decreasing at a constant pace—we enter the realm of **non-linear data** and **exponential
models**.

---

### What is an Exponential Model?

While a linear regression model assumes changes happen by adding a constant amount ($y = mx + b$), an **exponential model**
assumes changes happen by multiplying by a constant factor over equal intervals.

The general equation for an exponential growth model looks like this:

$$y = a \cdot b^x$$

_(Or written using the natural exponential base $e$: $y = a e^{kx}$)_

- **$a$**: The initial starting value (where $x = 0$).
- **$b$**: The growth factor (if $b > 1$, it's exponential **growth**; if $0 < b < 1$, it's exponential **decay**).
- **$x$**: The independent variable, typically representing time.

---

### Where Do We See Exponential Models?

Exponential relationships are everywhere in nature, finance, and technology:

1. **Compound Interest:** Money in a savings account grows exponentially because you earn interest on both your initial
   deposit _and_ the interest you've already accumulated.
2. **Population and Bacterial Growth:** Given unlimited resources, a colony of bacteria or a population will double in size
   at regular intervals, shooting upward rapidly.
3. **Technology Adoption / Viral Spread:** The spread of viral videos, social media trends, or early adoption curves of new
   technologies often follow an exponential path before leveling off.

---

### How Do We Fit and Evaluate Exponential Data?

Because straight lines don't fit curved data well, statisticians handle exponential data in a couple of ways:

- **Logarithmic Transformation:** By applying a logarithm to the dependent variable ($\ln(y)$), you can mathematically
  "straighten out" an exponential curve into a linear form. Once it's linear, you can use standard linear regression tools
  and evaluate it with $R^2$ as normal.
- **Non-Linear Least Squares (NLS):** Instead of transforming the data, advanced statistical software can fit the curved
  exponential equation directly to the raw data points by minimizing the squared errors.
