In statistics, **correlation** is a measure that describes the strength and direction of the relationship between two
variables. While regression looks at how to _predict_ one variable from another, correlation simply asks: **Do these two
variables move together, and if so, how closely?**

---

### The Correlation Coefficient ($r$)

The relationship between variables is measured using a number called the **correlation coefficient**, usually represented by
the letter **$r$**. This number always falls between **$-1$ and $+1$**:

- **$+1$ (Perfect Positive Correlation):** As one variable increases, the other variable increases at a consistent rate
  (e.g., hours spent studying and exam scores).
- **$0$ (No Correlation):** There is no relationship between the variables at all (e.g., a person's shoe size and their score
  on a history test).
- **$-1$ (Perfect Negative Correlation):** As one variable increases, the other variable decreases (e.g., the amount of fuel
  left in a car's tank and the number of miles driven).

---

### The Golden Rule: Correlation $\neq$ Causation

One of the most important concepts in data analysis is that **correlation does not imply causation**.

Just because two variables move together doesn't mean one causes the other to happen. For example, ice cream sales and shark
attacks are strongly correlated—both go up during the summer months. However, eating ice cream does not attract sharks; a
third hidden variable (hot weather and more people going to the beach) drives both!
