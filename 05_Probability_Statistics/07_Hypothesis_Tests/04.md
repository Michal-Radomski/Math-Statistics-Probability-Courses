A **t-test** (specifically, Student's t-test) is a fundamental statistical hypothesis test used to determine if there is a
significant difference between the means of two groups.

While it lives under the umbrella of statistics rather than pure probability, it relies heavily on probability distributions
(specifically, the **t-distribution**) to calculate the likelihood that the difference between groups happened by chance.

---

### When Do You Use a T-Test?

You use a t-test when you want to compare the averages (means) of two groups and answer the question: _Are these two groups
genuinely different, or is the variation just random noise?_

It is typically used when:

- You have a **small sample size** (usually under 30).
- The population standard deviation is **unknown**.

---

### The Three Main Types of T-Tests

1. **One-Sample T-Test:**

- Compares the mean of a single group against a known or target value (e.g., "Is the average test score of this specific
  class significantly different from the national average of 75?").

2. **Independent Two-Sample T-Test:**

- Compares the means of two completely separate, unrelated groups (e.g., "Do students who drink coffee score higher on exams
  on average than students who drink tea?").

3. **Paired Sample T-Test:**

- Compares means from the same group at two different times—often before and after an event (e.g., "What is the average blood
  pressure of patients _before_ taking a medication versus _after_ taking it?").

---

### How It Works: The T-Statistic and P-Value

The t-test calculates a number called the **t-value** (or t-statistic), which is basically a ratio:

$$\text{t-value} = \frac{\text{Difference between group means}}{\text{Variability within the groups (Standard Error)}}$$

- **A larger t-value** means the difference between the groups is much larger than the natural variation within them.
- **The p-value** tells you the probability that you would see a difference this large purely by random chance if there were
  actually no real difference between the groups. If the p-value is very small (typically less than $0.05$), scientists
  conclude the difference is **statistically significant**.
