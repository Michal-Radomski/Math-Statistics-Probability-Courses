**Linear algebra** is the branch of mathematics that studies **vectors**, **vector spaces**, **linear transformations**, and
**systems of linear equations**.

While regular algebra usually deals with single unknown numbers (like solving for $x$ in $2x + 3 = 7$), linear algebra scales
things up. It deals with entire collections of numbers organized into columns and rows—allowing us to solve massive sets of
equations all at once.

Think of linear algebra as the mathematics of **space, geometry, and data structure**.

---

### Key Building Blocks of Linear Algebra

1. **Vectors:**

- _In physics:_ A vector is an arrow that has both magnitude (length) and direction (like velocity or force).
- _In computer science/data:_ A vector is simply an ordered list of numbers (e.g., `[height, weight, age]`). It represents a
  single data point with multiple features.

2. **Matrices:**

- A matrix is a rectangular grid or table of numbers arranged in rows and columns.
- Matrices act like "machines" or rules that take in vectors and transform them—stretching them, rotating them, or shifting
  them in space.

3. **Systems of Linear Equations:**

- Finding the exact intersection point where multiple linear equations are all true at the same time.

---

### Why Does Linear Algebra Matter?

Linear algebra is the silent engine behind much of the digital world:

- **Machine Learning & AI:** Computers don't understand words, images, or sounds directly; they understand numbers. Images
  are stored as gigantic matrices of pixel values, and neural networks process data using heavy matrix multiplication.
- **Computer Graphics & Video Games:** Every 3D character, shadow, and camera angle in video games or CGI movies is
  manipulated using matrix transformations to rotate and project 3D objects onto a 2D screen.
- **Data Science:** Analyzing massive datasets, compressing images, and running search algorithms all rely on linear algebra
  to handle thousands of variables simultaneously.
