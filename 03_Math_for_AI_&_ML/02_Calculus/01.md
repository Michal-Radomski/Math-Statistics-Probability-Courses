Here is a breakdown of three of the most important rules in calculus for finding derivatives efficiently.

---

### 1. The Power Rule

The Power Rule is used when you need to differentiate a variable raised to a constant power. It brings the exponent to the
front and reduces the power by one.

- **Formula:**

$$\frac{d}{dx} [x^n] = n x^{n-1}$$

- **Example:** Find the derivative of $f(x) = x^4$.

$$f'(x) = 4x^{4-1} = 4x^3$$

---

### 2. The Product Rule

The Product Rule is used when a function is formed by the multiplication of two separate, differentiable functions ($u$ and
$v$). The derivative is the derivative of the first times the second, plus the first times the derivative of the second.

- **Formula:**

$$\frac{d}{dx}[u \cdot v] = u'v + uv'$$

_(In function notation: $(f \cdot g)' = f'g + fg'$)_

- **Example:** Find the derivative of $h(x) = x^2 \sin(x)$.
- Let $u = x^2$ and $v = \sin(x)$.
- Then $u' = 2x$ and $v' = \cos(x)$.
- Apply the formula:

$$h'(x) = (2x)(\sin(x)) + (x^2)(\cos(x)) = 2x \sin(x) + x^2 \cos(x)$$

---

### 3. The Chain Rule

The Chain Rule is used for **composite functions**—meaning a function nested inside another function (like $f(g(x))$). You
differentiate the outer function while leaving the inner function unchanged, then multiply by the derivative of the inner
function.

- **Formula:**

$$\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$$

- **Example:** Find the derivative of $h(x) = (3x^2 + 1)^4$.
- Outer function: $u^4$ (derivative is $4u^3$)
- Inner function: $g(x) = 3x^2 + 1$ (derivative is $6x$)
- Apply the formula:

$$h'(x) = 4(3x^2 + 1)^3 \cdot (6x) = 24x(3x^2 + 1)^3$$
