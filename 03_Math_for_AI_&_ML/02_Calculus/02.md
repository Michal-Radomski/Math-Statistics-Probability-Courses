**Implicit differentiation** is a technique used in calculus to find the derivative of a relation when the equation is not
easily solved explicitly for $y$ (i.e., when $y$ is not isolated on one side as $y = f(x)$).

For example, consider the equation of a circle:

$$x^2 + y^2 = 25$$

Instead of solving for $y$ (which results in $\pm\sqrt{25 - x^2}$ and creates two separate functions), we can differentiate
both sides of the equation directly with respect to $x$.

---

### Key Principle: Treat $y$ as a Function of $x$

The secret to implicit differentiation is remembering that **$y$ depends on $x$** ($y = y(x)$). Whenever you differentiate a
term containing $y$ with respect to $x$, you must apply the **Chain Rule** by multiplying by $\frac{dy}{dx}$.

- $\frac{d}{dx}[x^2] = 2x$
- $\frac{d}{dx}[y^2] = 2y \cdot \frac{dy}{dx}$ _(because of the Chain Rule!)_

---

### Step-by-Step Example

Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ for the circle equation: $x^2 + y^2 = 25$

1. **Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:**

$$\frac{d}{dx}[x^2 + y^2] = \frac{d}{dx}[25]$$

$$2x + 2y \frac{dy}{dx} = 0$$

2. **Isolate the terms with $\frac{dy}{dx}$ on one side:**

$$2y \frac{dy}{dx} = -2x$$

3. **Solve for $\frac{dy}{dx}$:**

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-2x}{2y} = -\frac{x}{y}$$

This tells us the slope of the tangent line at any point $(x, y)$ on the circle, even where $y$ isn't explicitly isolated!
