**Gradient descent** is one of the most fundamental optimization algorithms in machine learning and mathematics. Put simply,
it is a method used to find the minimum of a function—which, in machine learning, means finding the settings that result in
the lowest possible error (loss) for a model.

---

### The Hiking Analogy 🏔️

Imagine you are dropped blindfolded on a steep, foggy mountain, and your goal is to walk down to the lowest valley (where the
error is zero).

1. **Feel the slope:** You feel the ground with your feet to figure out which direction slopes downward the steepest.
2. **Take a step:** You take a step in that downward direction.
3. **Repeat:** You stop, feel the slope again, take another step, and repeat the process until the ground feels flat beneath
   your feet.

That process of sensing the slope and stepping downward is **gradient descent**. In math terms, the "slope" is the
**gradient** (derived using calculus), and "stepping" is updating the model's parameters.

---

### Key Concepts in Gradient Descent

- **The Cost Function (The Mountain):** A mathematical formula that measures how wrong your model's predictions are. High
  cost means bad predictions; low cost means great predictions.
- **The Learning Rate (Step Size):** This determines how big your steps are as you walk down the mountain:
- **Too high:** You take giant steps and might accidentally step right over the valley, bouncing back and forth forever
  without ever reaching the bottom.
- **Too low:** You take tiny baby steps, and while you will reach the bottom eventually, it might take forever.
