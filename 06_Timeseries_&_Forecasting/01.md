**Time series analysis and forecasting** form a core branch of data science and statistics focused on analyzing data points
collected over time to predict future trends.

---

### What is a Time Series?

A **time series** is a sequence of data points recorded at successive, equally spaced intervals in time (e.g., hourly, daily,
monthly, or yearly).

Unlike standard regression data—where observations are assumed to be independent of one another—time series data is
**chronologically dependent**. What happened yesterday directly influences or correlates with what happens today.

- **Examples:**
- Daily closing stock prices
- Hourly temperature readings
- Monthly retail sales figures
- Website traffic per minute

---

### What is Forecasting?

**Forecasting** is the process of using historical time series data, patterns, and statistical models to predict future
values.

While forecasting shares similarities with general machine learning prediction, it specifically deals with the **time
dimension**. You aren't just predicting an outcome based on static features; you are projecting how a metric will evolve over
future time steps.

---

### Key Components of a Time Series

To forecast accurately, analysts break down time series data into four primary components:

1. **Trend:** The long-term upward or downward movement in the data (e.g., the steady growth of global smartphone users over
   a decade).
2. **Seasonality:** Regular, periodic fluctuations that repeat over a fixed calendar period, such as daily, weekly, or yearly
   cycles (e.g., ice cream sales spiking every summer).
3. **Cyclicity:** Wavelike movements that occur over longer, irregular periods—often tied to broader economic cycles like
   recessions or booms.
4. **Noise (Irregularity):** Random, unpredictable variations in the data caused by short-term, unrepeatable events (e.g., a
   sudden storm temporarily dropping foot traffic).

---

### Common Forecasting Methods

- **Statistical Models:** Traditional methods like **ARIMA** (Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average) and **Exponential
  Smoothing** work well for stationary data with clear linear trends and seasonality.
- **Machine Learning Models:** Algorithms like XGBoost, Random Forests, or Gradient Boosting adapted with lag features.
- **Deep Learning Models:** Advanced architectures like **LSTM** (Long Short-Term Memory) networks and Transformers designed
  to capture complex, long-range sequential dependencies.
