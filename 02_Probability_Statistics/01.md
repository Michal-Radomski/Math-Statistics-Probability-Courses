Probability and statistics are two related but distinct branches of mathematics that deal with uncertainty, data, and
decision-making. Together, they form the foundation of how we make sense of random events and analyze information in fields
like science, business, sports, and technology.

---

### Probability

**Probability** is the study of **likelihood and chance**. It looks forward, starting with a known set of rules or conditions
and predicting how likely it is for certain events to happen.

- **How it works:** It measures the chance of an event occurring on a scale from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain), or 0% to
  100%.
- **Example:** If you flip a fair coin, the probability of getting heads is $0.5$ (or 50%). If you roll a standard six-sided
  die, the probability of rolling a 4 is $\frac{1}{6}$ (about 16.7%).

### Statistics

**Statistics** is the study of **data**. It works in reverse compared to probability: it looks backward, taking real-world
data from past events or samples to analyze patterns, draw conclusions, and make generalizations about a larger population.

- **How it works:** It involves collecting, organizing, analyzing, interpreting, and presenting data. Common tools include
  averages (mean, median, mode), graphs, and margin of error.
- **Example:** If a video game developer tracks how many players complete a specific level over a weekend, they are using
  statistics to understand player behavior and game difficulty.

---

### How They Work Together

While they are separate fields, they rely heavily on each other:

1. **Probability** tells you what to expect based on theoretical models (e.g., "If I draw cards from a shuffled deck, I
   should expect a heart 25% of the time").
2. **Statistics** checks reality against those expectations using actual data (e.g., "I drew 100 cards and got 30 hearts; is
   the deck biased, or is this normal variation?").
