In probability and statistics, the **normal distribution**—often called the **bell curve** or **Gaussian distribution**—is
the most important and widely used probability distribution.

It describes data that clusters around a central mean. When you plot it, it creates a symmetric, bell-shaped curve where most
observations pile up near the middle, and frequencies taper off equally on both sides.

---

### Key Characteristics of a Normal Distribution

1. **Symmetry:** The left and right halves of the curve are mirror images of each other.
2. **The Center:** The **mean** ($\mu$), **median**, and **mode** all share the exact same value right at the peak in the
   center.
3. **Asymptotic:** The tails of the curve get closer and closer to the horizontal axis, but they technically _never touch it_
   (infinite range).
4. **Defined by Two Parameters:**

- **Mean ($\mu$):** Determines where the center of the curve sits on the number line.
- **Standard Deviation ($\sigma$):** Determines how "wide" or "flat" the bell curve is (measuring how spread out the data
  is).

---

### The Empirical Rule (The 68-95-99.7 Rule)

One of the most useful rules of thumb in statistics applies directly to any normal distribution. No matter what the mean or
standard deviation are, the data always follows this exact spread relative to standard deviations ($\sigma$) from the mean:

- **68%** of the data falls within **1 standard deviation** of the mean ($\mu \pm 1\sigma$).
- **95%** of the data falls within **2 standard deviations** of the mean ($\mu \pm 2\sigma$).
- **99.7%** of the data falls within **3 standard deviations** of the mean ($\mu \pm 3\sigma$).

> **Example:** If human heights are normally distributed with a mean of 170 cm and a standard deviation of 10 cm, 95% of
> people will have heights between 150 cm and 190 cm ($\mu \pm 2\sigma$).

---

### The Standard Normal Distribution and $Z$-Scores

Because every normal distribution has different means and standard deviations, statisticians created a standardized version
called the **Standard Normal Distribution**, which always has:

- A mean ($\mu$) of **0**
- A standard deviation ($\sigma$) of **1**

To figure out where any individual data point ($X$) falls on this standard curve, you convert it into a **$Z$-score** using
this formula:

$$Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}$$

- **$X$** = your specific value
- **$\mu$** = the population mean
- **$\sigma$** = the standard deviation
- **$Z$** = how many standard deviations your value is away from the mean.
