In probability and statistics, a **binomial distribution** is a discrete probability distribution that summarizes the
likelihood that a value will take one of two independent values (like success/failure, heads/tails, or yes/no) under a given
number of parameters.

It is the exact distribution you use when you want to know the probability of getting a specific number of "successes" out of
a set number of independent trials.

---

### The 4 Conditions of a Binomial Experiment (BINS)

Before you can use the binomial formula, your scenario must meet four conditions:

1. **Binary outcomes:** Each trial has only two possible outcomes (Success or Failure).
2. **Independent trials:** The result of one trial does not affect the outcome of any other trial.
3. **Number of trials is fixed:** You must decide beforehand exactly how many times you will run the experiment ($n$).
4. **Same probability:** The probability of success ($p$) must be identical for every single trial.

---

### The Binomial Formula

To find the probability of getting exactly $k$ successes out of $n$ trials, the formula is:

$$P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}$$

- **$n$** = total number of trials
- **$k$** = target number of successful outcomes you are looking for
- **$p$** = probability of success on a single trial
- **$1 - p$** = probability of failure on a single trial (sometimes written as $q$)
- **$\binom{n}{k}$** = The combination formula we looked at earlier! It calculates how many different ways those $k$
  successes can be arranged among the $n$ trials.

---

### Practical Example: Flipping a Coin

Imagine you flip a fair coin **5 times** ($n = 5$). What is the probability that you get **exactly 3 heads** ($k = 3$)?

#### Step 1: Identify your variables

- Number of trials: $n = 5$
- Target successes: $k = 3$ (getting heads)
- Probability of success on one flip: $p = 0.5$
- Probability of failure (tails): $1 - p = 0.5$

#### Step 2: Plug into the formula

$$P(X = 3) = \binom{5}{3} (0.5)^3 (0.5)^{5-3}$$

#### Step 3: Calculate each part

1. **The Combinations ($\binom{5}{3}$):**

$$\frac{5!}{3!(5-3)!} = \frac{120}{6 \times 2} = 10 \text{ ways}$$

2. **The Successes ($0.5^3$):** $0.5 \times 0.5 \times 0.5 = 0.125$
3. **The Failures ($0.5^2$):** $0.5 \times 0.5 = 0.25$

#### Step 4: Multiply them together

$$P(X = 3) = 10 \times 0.125 \times 0.25 = 0.3125 \text{ (or } 31.25\% \text{)}$$

There is a **31.25% chance** of getting exactly 3 heads if you flip a coin 5 times.
