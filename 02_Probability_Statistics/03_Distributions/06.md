A **$Z$-score** (also called a **standard score**) is a statistical measurement that describes a value's relationship to the
mean of a group of values. Specifically, a $Z$-score tells you **how many standard deviations a given data point is above or
below the mean**.

Once you convert your data into $Z$-scores, you are using the **Standard Normal Distribution**, which always has a mean of
**0** and a standard deviation of **1**. This makes it easy to compare data from completely different datasets.

---

### The $Z$-Score Formula

To calculate the $Z$-score of any data point, use this formula:

$$Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}$$

- **$X$** = the specific value or data point you are looking at
- **$\mu$** = the mean (average) of the dataset
- **$\sigma$** = the standard deviation of the dataset
- **$Z$** = the resulting $Z$-score

---

### How to Interpret a $Z$-Score

The sign and number of the $Z$-score tell you everything you need to know about where that value stands:

- **$Z = 0$**: Your value is **exactly at the mean**.
- **Positive $Z$-score (e.g., $+1.5$)**: Your value is **above the mean** (1.5 standard deviations higher than average).
- **Negative $Z$-score (e.g., $-2.0$)**: Your value is **below the mean** (2.0 standard deviations lower than average).

---

### Practical Example: Comparing Apples to Oranges

Imagine you take two different tests in school, and you want to know which one you did better on relative to the rest of your
classmates:

- **Math Test:** You scored **85**. The class average ($\mu$) was **70**, with a standard deviation ($\sigma$) of **10**.
- **History Test:** You scored **60**. The class average ($\mu$) was **50**, with a standard deviation ($\sigma$) of **5**.

Which grade was actually more impressive? Let's calculate the $Z$-score for both:

1. **Math $Z$-score:**

$$Z = \frac{85 - 70}{10} = \frac{15}{10} = +1.5$$

_(Your math score was 1.5 standard deviations above the average.)_ 2. **History $Z$-score:**

$$Z = \frac{60 - 50}{5} = \frac{10}{5} = +2.0$$

_(Your history score was 2.0 standard deviations above the average.)_

> **Conclusion:** Even though your raw score in math was higher (85 vs 60), you actually performed better relative to your
> classmates in **history** ($Z = +2.0$), because your score was further out on the positive end of the distribution curve.
