In combinatorics, **combinations with repetition** (also known as _combinations with replacement_) refer to selecting items
from a pool where **items can be chosen more than once**, and **order does not matter**.

For example, if you are choosing scoops of ice cream from a list of flavors and you want three scoops of chocolate,
repetition is allowed.

---

### The Formula for Combinations with Repetition

To find the number of ways to choose $r$ items from a set of $n$ distinct types with repetition allowed, the formula is:

$$C_{\text{rep}}(n, r) = \binom{n + r - 1}{r} = \frac{(n + r - 1)!}{r!(n - 1)!}$$

- $n$ = number of distinct categories or items available to choose from
- $r$ = total number of items being selected
- $!$ = factorial

---

### Example: Choosing Ice Cream Scoops

Imagine you are ordering a 3-scoop ice cream cone ($r = 3$), and there are 4 available flavors to choose from ($n = 4$:
Chocolate, Vanilla, Strawberry, Mint). You can mix and match, or pick all three scoops of the exact same flavor.

Plug $n = 4$ and $r = 3$ into the formula:

$$\binom{4 + 3 - 1}{3} = \binom{6}{3}$$

Now, calculate using the factorial formula:

$$\binom{6}{3} = \frac{6!}{3!(6 - 3)!} = \frac{6!}{3! \times 3!} = \frac{720}{6 \times 6} = \frac{720}{36} = 20 \text{ possible combinations}$$
