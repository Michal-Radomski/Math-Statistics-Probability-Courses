In probability, **conditional probability** is the likelihood of an event occurring _given that another event has already
occurred_. It updates our probability calculation based on new information or restricted sample spaces.

- **Notation:** $P(A \mid B)$ (read as "the probability of event $A$ _given_ event $B$")

---

### The Formula for Conditional Probability

To find the probability of $A$ given $B$, you divide the probability that **both** events occur (their intersection) by the
probability that the given event ($B$) occurs:

$$P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$$

_(Note: This assumes that $P(B) > 0$, because you cannot condition on an impossible event.)_

---

### Step-by-Step Example: Drawing Marbles

Imagine a bag containing **5 marbles**: 3 are red and 2 are blue.

1. You reach in and pull out a red marble without replacing it.
2. Now, what is the probability of drawing a **second red marble** given that the first one was red?

- **Step 1:** Find $P(B)$ — the probability of the first event (drawing a red marble initially):

$$P(\text{1st Red}) = \frac{3}{5}$$

- **Step 2:** Find $P(A \cap B)$ — the probability of drawing _two_ red marbles in a row:

$$P(\text{Both Red}) = \frac{3}{5} \times \frac{2}{4} = \frac{6}{20} = \frac{3}{10}$$

- **Step 3:** Apply the conditional probability formula:

$$P(\text{2nd Red} \mid \text{1st Red}) = \frac{P(\text{Both Red})}{P(\text{1st Red})} = \frac{\frac{3}{10}}{\frac{3}{5}} = \frac{3}{10} \times \frac{5}{3} = \frac{1}{2}$$

- _Intuitive shortcut:_ Since one red marble is already gone, there are now only 4 marbles left in the bag, and only 2 of
  them are red. So $\frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$.

---

### The Multiplication Rule (Rearranging the Formula)

If you rearrange the conditional probability formula, you get the **Multiplication Rule**, which is used to find the
probability of _two dependent events happening together_:

$$P(A \cap B) = P(B) \times P(A \mid B)$$
