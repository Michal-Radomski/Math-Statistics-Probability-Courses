In combinatorics, the term **variations** (sometimes called _arrangements_) is often used interchangeably with
**permutations**, or specifically to describe situations where you select $r$ items from a set of $n$ items and **order
matters**.

Depending on the textbook or curriculum, variations are generally split into two types: **variations without repetition** and
**variations with repetition**.

---

### 1. Variations Without Repetition (Ordered Subsets)

This is the exact same concept as the standard permutation formula where you choose $r$ items from $n$ distinct items without
replacement, and the order of selection matters.

$$V(n, r) = P(n, r) = \frac{n!}{(n - r)!}$$

- $n$ = total number of items available
- $r$ = number of items selected and ordered

**Example:** Selecting a 1st, 2nd, and 3rd place winner from 10 runners.

$$V(10, 3) = \frac{10!}{(10 - 3)!} = \frac{10!}{7!} = 10 \times 9 \times 8 = 720 \text{ ways}$$

---

### 2. Variations With Repetition

When items _can_ be chosen more than once, and order still matters, it is called a **variation with repetition**. This
matches the formula for permutations with repetition.

$$V_{\text{rep}}(n, r) = n^r$$

- $n$ = total number of items available to choose from
- $r$ = number of positions or selections being made

**Example:** Creating a 3-letter password using the 26 letters of the alphabet, where letters can be repeated.

$$V_{\text{rep}}(26, 3) = 26^3 = 17,576 \text{ possibilities}$$

---

### Summary Table of All Three Concepts

| Concept          | Order Matters? | Repetition Allowed? | Formula                       | Common Use Case                                |
| ---------------- | -------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Permutations** | Yes            | No                  | $n!$ (or $\frac{n!}{(n-r)!}$) | Arranging all books on a shelf, race standings |
| **Variations**   | Yes            | No or Yes           | $\frac{n!}{(n-r)!}$ or $n^r$  | Ordered selections, PIN codes, passwords       |
| **Combinations** | No             | No                  | $\frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}$         | Card hands, lottery tickets, committees        |
