**Bayes' Theorem** is a powerful formula that allows us to "reverse" conditional probabilities. If you know the probability
of an effect given a cause ($P(Effect \mid Cause)$), Bayes' Theorem lets you calculate the probability of the cause given the
effect ($P(Cause \mid Effect)$).

It is widely used in everything from medical testing and spam filters to machine learning and AI.

---

### The Formula for Bayes' Theorem

$$P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(B \mid A) \times P(A)}{P(B)}$$

- **$P(A \mid B)$** = **Posterior probability:** The probability of event $A$ occurring given that $B$ is true (what we want
  to find).
- **$P(B \mid A)$** = **Likelihood:** The probability of event $B$ occurring given that $A$ is true.
- **$P(A)$** = **Prior probability:** The initial probability of event $A$ happening before considering any new evidence.
- **$P(B)$** = **Marginal probability:** The total probability of event $B$ happening across all possible scenarios.

Often, the denominator $P(B)$ is expanded using the **Law of Total Probability** for two mutually exclusive outcomes ($A$ and
its complement $A'$):

$$P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(B \mid A) \times P(A)}{[P(B \mid A) \times P(A)] + [P(B \mid A') \times P(A')]}$$

---

### Practical Example: The Rare Disease Test

Imagine a rare disease that affects **1% of the population**. A pharmaceutical company creates a test for this disease with
the following accuracy:

- If a person **has** the disease, the test is **99% accurate** (True Positive: $0.99$).
- If a person **does not have** the disease, the test has a **2% false positive rate** (False Positive: $0.02$).

**Question:** If a randomly selected person tests **positive**, what is the actual probability that they have the disease?

#### Step 1: Identify our variables

- Event $A$: Having the disease $\rightarrow P(A) = 0.01$ (and not having it $P(A') = 0.99$)
- Event $B$: Testing positive $\rightarrow$
- Probability of testing positive _if you have the disease_: $P(B \mid A) = 0.99$
- Probability of testing positive _if you don't have the disease_: $P(B \mid A') = 0.02$

#### Step 2: Plug the numbers into Bayes' Theorem

$$P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(B \mid A) \times P(A)}{[P(B \mid A) \times P(A)] + [P(B \mid A') \times P(A')]}$$

$$P(\text{Disease} \mid \text{Positive}) = \frac{0.99 \times 0.01}{(0.99 \times 0.01) + (0.02 \times 0.99)}$$

#### Step 3: Calculate

- Numerator (True Positives): $0.99 \times 0.01 = 0.0099$
- False Positives: $0.02 \times 0.99 = 0.0198$
- Total Positive Tests ($P(B)$): $0.0099 + 0.0198 = 0.0297$

$$\frac{0.0099}{0.0297} = \frac{1}{3} \approx 33.3\%$$

> **Surprising Result:** Even though the test is 99% accurate, if you test positive for a rare disease, there is only a
> **33.3% chance** you actually have it. This happens because the disease is so rare that the pool of false positives from
> the healthy population outweighs the true positives!
