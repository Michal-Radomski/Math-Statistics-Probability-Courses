In probability and combinatorics, **permutations** and **combinations** are ways to count the number of possible outcomes or
arrangements. The key difference between them comes down to one question: **Does the order of the items matter?**

---

### 1. Permutations (Order Matters)

A permutation is an arrangement of items in a **specific, distinct order**. If you rearrange the items, it counts as a
different permutation.

For example, the lock combination on a locker is technically a permutation because entering $1-2-3$ is different from
$3-2-1$.

#### Formula

The number of ways to choose and arrange $r$ items from a set of $n$ items is given by:

$$P(n, r) = \frac{n!}{(n - r)!}$$

Where:

- $n$ = total number of items in the set
- $r$ = number of items to choose and arrange
- $!$ = factorial (e.g., $4! = 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1 = 24$)

---

### 2. Combinations (Order Does NOT Matter)

A combination is a selection of items where **order does not matter**. If you pick the same items in a different sequence, it
counts as the exact same combination.

For example, if you are dealt a hand of cards in poker, the order in which the cards are handed to you doesn't change the
hand you hold.

#### Formula

The number of ways to choose $r$ items from a set of $n$ items (where order doesn't matter) is:

$$C(n, r) = \binom{n}{r} = \frac{n!}{r!(n - r)!}$$

---

### Quick Comparison Example

Imagine you have 3 fruits: **Apple ($A$)**, **Banana ($B$)**, and **Cherry ($C$)**, and you want to pick **2 of them**.

- **Permutations (Order matters):** $AB$, $BA$, $AC$, $CA$, $BC$, $CB$ ($6$ total ways)
- **Combinations (Order doesn't matter):** $AB$ (same as $BA$), $AC$ (same as $CA$), $BC$ (same as $CB$) ($3$ total ways)
