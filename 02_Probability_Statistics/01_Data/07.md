**Quartiles** and the **Interquartile Range (IQR)** are statistical measures used to describe the spread, center, and
distribution of a dataset. They are especially useful because, unlike the mean and standard deviation, they are not easily
distorted by extreme values or outliers.

---

### Quartiles

Quartiles divide an ordered dataset into four equal parts, with each part representing 25% of the data.

- **First Quartile ($Q_1$) / 25th Percentile:** The median of the lower half of the data. 25% of the data points lie below
  this value, and 75% lie above it.
- **Second Quartile ($Q_2$) / Median / 50th Percentile:** The exact middle of the dataset. 50% of the data points lie below
  this value, and 50% lie above it.
- **Third Quartile ($Q_3$) / 75th Percentile:** The median of the upper half of the data. 75% of the data points lie below
  this value, and 25% lie above it.

---

### Interquartile Range (IQR)

The **Interquartile Range** measures the statistical dispersion or spread of the middle 50% of a dataset. It focuses entirely
on the central portion of the data, ignoring the highest and lowest 25%.

**Formula:**

$$\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$$

- **Why it matters:** Because it only looks at the range between the first and third quartiles, the IQR is considered a
  **robust measure of variability**. It is unaffected by outliers or skewed data distributions, making it ideal for skewed
  datasets (like household incomes or house prices).
- **Outlier Detection:** The IQR is commonly used to flag outliers in a dataset using the "1.5 IQR rule". Any data point that
  falls below $Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ or above $Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ is typically considered an outlier.

---

### How to Calculate Quartiles and IQR (Step-by-Step)

Consider the following ordered dataset of 8 numbers: `3, 7, 8, 12, 14, 15, 18, 20`

1. **Find the median ($Q_2$):** Since there is an even number of values, take the average of the two middle numbers (12 and
   14).

$$Q_2 = \frac{12 + 14}{2} = 13$$

2. **Split the data into lower and upper halves:**

- Lower half: `3, 7, 8, 12`
- Upper half: `14, 15, 18, 20`

3. **Find $Q_1$ and $Q_3$:**

- $Q_1$ is the median of the lower half (`7` and `8`): $\frac{7 + 8}{2} = 7.5$
- $Q_3$ is the median of the upper half (`15` and `18`): $\frac{15 + 18}{2} = 16.5$

4. **Calculate the IQR:**

$$\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 = 16.5 - 7.5 = 9$$
